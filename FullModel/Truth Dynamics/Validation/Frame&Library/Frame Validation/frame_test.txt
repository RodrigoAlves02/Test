FRAME VALIDATION - CHOOSING THE PROPAGATION FRAME FOR THE TRUTH DYNAMICS
FullModel / Truth Dynamics / Validation / Frame Validation
Script: frame_test.py        Results: frame_test.json
Recorded 2026-09-16


================================================================================
1. PURPOSE
================================================================================

The truth dynamics can be propagated either in the inertial frame or in the
body-fixed rotating frame. The two are physically equivalent, so the choice is
made on cost and on numerical accuracy.

The cost asymmetry comes from the gravity model. The polyhedral (Werner-Scheeres)
library accepts a position in the BODY-FIXED frame and returns an acceleration in
that same frame. So:

  - Propagating in the INERTIAL frame, every evaluation must rotate the position
    into the body frame before calling the field, and rotate the returned
    acceleration back out into the inertial frame.
        -> 2 vector rotations per evaluation.
    Both use the same rotation angle, so one sine/cosine pair serves the pair.

  - Propagating in the ROTATING frame, no rotation is needed because the position
    is already in the body frame, but the frame is non-inertial and every
    evaluation owes the Coriolis and centrifugal terms:
        -2 w x v        (1 cross product)
        -w x (w x r)    (2 cross products)
        -> 3 cross products per evaluation.

With RK4 the stage count is 4 evaluations per step. This was not assumed, it was
measured: the run reports 1440 evaluations over 360 steps, exactly 4.0 per step.
Per STEP the totals are therefore

        inertial   4 evaluations x 2 rotations       =  8 rotations
        rotating   4 evaluations x 3 cross products  = 12 cross products

(Note: an earlier count of 5 evaluations per step would give 10 and 15. The
classical RK4 tableau has four stages - k1, k2, k3, k4 - and the measurement
confirms four.)


================================================================================
2. METHOD
================================================================================

A state on a circular orbit of RADIUS 35 km is propagated over a short arc
(6 hours, about a third of a revolution) with RK4 at a deliberately coarse fixed
step of 60 s, once in each frame, from the same initial condition converted
between frames.

TIMING
  The time spent inside the frame algebra and the time spent inside the gravity
  call are accumulated separately, and divided by the number of evaluations to
  give a PER-EVALUATION AVERAGE. The average is the quantity that matters: a
  total depends on how long the arc is, whereas a per-evaluation figure is a
  property of the frame and can be multiplied by whatever stage count any other
  integrator happens to have.

  perf_counter costs order 100 ns per call against frame work of order 1 us, so
  the instrumented figures carry a few percent of timer overhead and are an upper
  bound. An uninstrumented microbenchmark of the same operations is run
  afterwards as a cross-check; the two agree to within about 10%.

GRAVITY MODEL USED FOR THE TIMING
  A POINT MASS, not the polyhedral mesh, for two reasons.

  (a) Resolution. The polyhedral evaluation costs about 355 us. Against that the
      frame algebra is 0.5-1.1% of the runtime and the difference between the two
      frames is buried in noise. With a point mass at about 1 us the frame algebra
      becomes 29-45% of the runtime and the comparison is actually measurable.

  (b) An exact reference. A point mass on a circular orbit has a closed-form
      solution - uniform rotation in the orbit plane at n = sqrt(mu/R^3) - so both
      frames can be checked against analytic truth instead of only against each
      other. No numerical reference trajectory is needed and the reference carries
      no error of its own.

  IMPORTANT: the two rotations are STILL PERFORMED in the inertial path when the
  point mass is used. The code path is unchanged and only the field function is
  swapped. A point mass is spherically symmetric, so rotating the position in and
  the acceleration back out is a mathematical no-op - the rotation matrix is
  orthogonal and the round trip is exact to machine precision, giving numerically
  the same answer as evaluating directly in inertial. The rotations are retained
  purely so that the measured COST remains representative of what the body-fixed
  polyhedral field will demand in production. They are timed, they are real
  operations, and they do not affect the answer.

  A consequence worth keeping: because the point mass is frame-invariant, the
  accuracy difference reported below is a pure frame-and-integrator effect with no
  gravity-model contribution whatsoever.

CORRECTNESS CHECK, RUN FIRST
  A trajectory propagated in one frame and then converted must equal the
  trajectory propagated in the other. If that fails no timing means anything. The
  conversions used are

        r_I = Rz(wt) r_B                  r_B = Rz(-wt) r_I
        v_I = Rz(wt) (v_B + w x r_B)      v_B = Rz(-wt) v_I - w x r_B

  and the round trip of the initial state closes to 4.7e-16.


================================================================================
3. RESULTS
================================================================================

Seed 20260916, R = 35 km, dt = 60 s, span 21600 s, RK4, point-mass gravity.

     frame   steps   evals  evals/step  total [s]  frame us/eval  grav us/eval  frame %
  inertial     360    1440         4.0     0.0041          0.829         0.822    28.9
  rotating     360    1440         4.0     0.0051          1.595         0.821    44.8

  uninstrumented microbenchmark:  inertial 0.746 us, rotating 1.382 us per evaluation

COST
  The inertial frame costs 0.829 us per evaluation in frame algebra against the
  rotating frame's 1.595 us - a factor of 1.9, which is what the operation counts
  predict (2 rotations sharing one sine/cosine pair, against 3 cross products).
  Over a whole step that is 3.3 us against 6.4 us.

  In production with the polyhedral field at ~355 us per evaluation this
  difference is 0.9% of the runtime and is not a deciding factor on its own.

ACCURACY, against the exact circular solution
  inertial : 1.77e-06 m
  rotating : 1.72e-03 m      (converted to inertial before comparing)

  The inertial frame is about 1000 times more accurate at the same step size.

  The mechanism is the angular rate the integrator has to resolve. In the inertial
  frame the vehicle traverses a circle at n = 1.02e-4 rad/s. In the rotating frame
  it sees that motion combined with the body rotation w = 3.31e-4 rad/s, so the
  apparent angular rate is 2.2 to 4.2 times higher depending on the orbit
  orientation relative to the spin axis. RK4 local error scales as the fifth power
  of (angular rate x step), which predicts a penalty between 56x and 1370x. The
  measured ratio is 972x, inside that range.

  Note what this reveals: the agreement between the two frames, 1.72e-3 m, is not
  a shared truncation error. It is the ROTATING frame's error alone, since the
  inertial result is three orders of magnitude closer to truth. Without the
  analytic solution there would have been no way to tell which of the two was
  wrong.


================================================================================
4. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The truth dynamics are propagated in the INERTIAL frame.

  - Cheaper: 1.9x less frame algebra per evaluation, because two rotations sharing
    one sine/cosine pair cost less than three cross products.
  - Far more accurate: ~1000x at equal step size on an orbital arc, because the
    rotating frame forces the integrator to resolve the body rotation on top of
    the orbital motion.
  - It is also the frame the sensors work in, so no conversion sits in the
    measurement path.


================================================================================
5. NOTES ON SCOPE
================================================================================

This test was run to answer one question: which frame is FASTER. It answers it.
The rotating frame's three cross products per evaluation cost 1.9 times the
inertial frame's two rotations, so the extra operations the rotating frame
requires are not necessary - the inertial frame simply does not need them, and is
cheaper for it.

The accuracy result is a second finding that came out of having an exact
reference available, and it points the same way, so nothing about the decision is
in tension.

The descent could be run through the same script out of curiosity, since a
descent trajectory is nearly stationary in the rotating frame while the landing
site sweeps about 34 degrees through the inertial frame over 1800 s. That is an
interesting comparison but it is not a condition on this result: the operation
counts and their costs do not change with the trajectory.

SRP and third-body perturbations are not included here. Both are naturally
expressed in the inertial frame, so adding them can only widen the gap in the
inertial frame's favour.
