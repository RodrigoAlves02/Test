SOURCE

  Approved for Public Release; Distribution Unlimited; #23-1041; 07/11/23.

  NOTE ON EXTRACTION: this PDF is CID-encoded (subset FuturaMaxiStd fonts, no
  plain text layer).  pypdf / pdftotext / poppler are all unavailable or return
  zero characters.  I decoded it by parsing the PDF objects, decompressing the
  content streams and rebuilding the ToUnicode CMaps by hand.  The extractor is
  saved at  scratchpad/pdftxt.py  and can be re-run to re-verify every number
  below.  Nothing here is from a secondary source.

WHY THIS UNIT  (this is the defensible part, not just the numbers)
  The datasheet's own heritage list, quoted verbatim, includes:
      Deep Space I, TSX-5, MightySat II, Muses-C, Coriolis, Terra SAR-X,
      Kepler Space Telescope, WISE Space Telescope, Ladee, Planet-C,
      Clementine, Hayabusa2, Mars Rover (Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity,
      and now Perseverance)
  Muses-C is Hayabusa 1.  So the LN-200S is the flown IMU on BOTH Japanese
  asteroid sample-return missions.  The datasheet also states:
      [Q] "The unit is hermetically sealed, which is advantageous for
           planetary and asteroid probes."
      [Q] "The LN-200S has the lowest gyro and accelerometer white noise in
           the medium accuracy IMU class."
  The alternative anchor -- the Honeywell MIMU flown on OSIRIS-REx -- is a dead
  end: [X] the datasheet PDF is image-only (pypdf extracts 0 chars) and
  SatCatalog lists only dry mass 4.08 kg and average power 34.0 W.  No
  sensor-level specs for the MIMU exist in public literature.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UNIT CONVERSION AND CONSEQUENCES   (g0 = 9.80665 m/s^2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [D] bias  300 ug   = 300e-6 * 9.80665        = 2.9420e-03 m/s^2
  [D] noise  35 ug/sqrt(Hz)                    = 3.4323e-04 (m/s^2)/sqrt(Hz)

  Discrete per-sample sigma for a white-noise PSD N is  sigma_a = N/sqrt(dt):
  [D]   dt = 1/400 s (datasheet rate)  ->  6.865e-03 m/s^2
  [D]   dt = 0.5   s (our sub-step)    ->  4.854e-04 m/s^2
  [D]   dt = 1.0   s                   ->  3.432e-04 m/s^2

  Velocity random walk accumulated over the whole 2052 s flight:
  [D]   sigma_dv = N*sqrt(T) = 3.4323e-4 * sqrt(2052) = 0.0155 m/s
        -> NOISE IS IRRELEVANT at our accuracy target.

  Deterministic error terms, evaluated on the true specific force:
  [D]   scale factor 300 ppm  on 0.0333 m/s^2  ->  1.00e-05 m/s^2
  [D]   misalignment 0.1 mrad on 0.0333 m/s^2  ->  3.33e-06 m/s^2
        -> BOTH NEGLIGIBLE.  They are currently in imu.py and earn nothing;
           they can stay for realism but they do not drive anything.

  BIAS IS THE ENTIRE ERROR BUDGET:
  [D]   2.9420e-03 m/s^2  =   8.8 % of full-thrust acceleration (20 N/600 kg)
  [D]                     =  35.3 % of min-thrust  acceleration ( 5 N/600 kg)
  [D]                     =    53 % of Eros surface gravity
  [D]   if left unestimated over 2052 s:
            velocity error  b*T          =    6.04 m/s
            position error  0.5*b*T^2    =    6195 m
        -> This is the quantitative justification for the 3 bias states in the
           EKF.  It is not a refinement, it is the difference between landing
           and missing the asteroid.  It is also the reason a 35-minute
           inertial-only coast is impossible and Hayabusa2/OSIRIS-REx both
           fly optical + ranging updates.

GAP I CANNOT CLOSE FROM THE DATASHEET
  [X] In-run bias stability and accelerometer bias random walk / correlation
      time are NOT on the datasheet.  "Bias repeatability" is a turn-on to
      turn-on figure.  So:
        - 300 ug legitimately sets the initial bias draw and the P0 bias block;
        - the Gauss-Markov correlation time tau in ekf.py remains an
          engineering assumption and must be declared as such in the thesis.
      Two honest options:
        (a) model the bias as a random CONSTANT (tau -> infinity).  This is
            actually the literal reading of "repeatability": one draw per
            turn-on, fixed for the run.  Cleanest to defend.
        (b) pick a finite tau, state it is an assumption, and show the landing
            result is insensitive to it over a plausible range.

  [Q] ONC-W1 focal length 10.35 mm ; ONC-W2 focal length 10.44 mm
  [Q] ONC-T: "6.35 x 6.35 deg. FOV", focal length 121.1 mm

  CONSISTENCY CHECK that makes the [S] value safe to use:
  [D]   65.24 deg at f = 10.35 mm over 1024 px implies a pitch of
        2*10.35*tan(32.62 deg)/1024 = 12.94 um.
        Two independent quantities agree with the quoted 13 um to 0.5 %.

  CONSEQUENCE FOR OUR DESIGN
  [D]   pixel scale = 65.24/1024 = 0.06371 deg/px = 1.1120 mrad/px
  The current config_nav.py value CAM_FOV_DEG = 45.0 is invented and should
  become 65.24.  It also matters geometrically: ONC-W1 is body-fixed to the
  spacecraft BOTTOM panel looking along -z, i.e. exactly the "camera along the
  thrust axis" in our two-camera layout, so the flown geometry matches the
  architecture already chosen.

2b.  MEASUREMENT NOISE -- NASA GSFC autoNGC / GIANT

SOURCE
  Shoemaker et al., "Terrain Relative Navigation in a Lunar Landing Scenario

  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210024481/downloads/

  [Q] Section IV: "The TRN measurement rate is set at one surface image per
      minute, with 3 to 5 landmarks per image, and the measurement noise is
      set at 0.1 pixels 1-sigma in each image pixel column/row direction."

  [Q] Section V, measured against ray-traced synthetic LRO NAC imagery through
      their GIANT onboard image processor:
        correlation score 0.997
        landmark pixel error (col,row)_err = (0.025, 0.169) pixels
        "the errors are approximately zero-mean, indicating no significant
         biases in the current setup"
        "serves as a useful approximation of the lower-bound on the achievable
         measurement accuracy"

  [Q] Table 3, NAVCAM definition (their camera, not ours -- recorded so the
      transfer is explicit): detector 1440(H) x 1080(V) px, pixel size 3.45 um,
      focal length 5.27 mm, 8 bits/pixel.

  WHY 0.1 px TRANSFERS TO ONC-W1: it is an image-processing figure (correlation
  of a rendered maplet against the image), independent of the optics.  The
  optics enter only through the pixel scale, which we take from ONC-W1.

  [Q] Table 2 also gives their filter uncertainty at 15 km altitude with
      TRN+GPS (3-sigma): radial 1.6 m, in-track 0.8 m, cross-track 0.6 m;
      velocity 2.5 / 1.0 / 1.0 mm/s.  Lunar + GPS, so not directly ours, but
      it is the order of magnitude a bearing-based filter reaches.

  CONSEQUENCE FOR OUR DESIGN
  [D]   0.1 px * 1.1120 mrad/px = 111.2 urad 1-sigma per axis
  [D]   cross-range position error contributed by one bearing, = h * 111.2urad:
            11 771 m altitude  ->  1.309 m
             5 000 m           ->  0.556 m
             1 000 m           ->  0.111 m
               100 m           ->  0.011 m
                10 m           ->  0.0011 m
  This is the anisotropy that was missing from perception.py.  A bearing is
  sharp ACROSS the line of sight and blind ALONG it, and it sharpens linearly
  during descent.  The current "camera returns a position fix" model has none
  of that structure.

  FLIGHT CORROBORATION
  OSIRIS-REx Natural Feature Tracking, "Autonomous Navigation Performance Using
  Natural Feature Tracking during the OSIRIS-REx TAG Sample Collection Event",
  doi 10.3847/PSJ/ac5183
  [Q] Section 4.2 / Fig. 24: 1-sigma position uncertainty ~1.2 m at Checkpoint
  [Q] Abstract: delivered within 1 m of the target, 3.5 cm and 1.4 s from the
      predicted TAG point
  [Q] Section 4.1.2: NFT-predicted feature locations within 50 cm
  [Q] Section 4.1.1: ground sample distance <2 cm/pixel, often <=1 cm/pixel on
      final approach
  The 1.2 m flown figure sits right on the 1.31 m the bearing model predicts at
  our handover altitude, which is the strongest single cross-check in this file.

  Camera hardware, for reference (TAGCAMS NavCam 2 did the NFT imaging):
  Bos et al. (2018), Space Science Reviews, doi 10.1007/s11214-017-0465-2
  [Q] NavCam FOV 44 x 32 deg, pixel scale 0.28 mrad/pixel, 2592x1944 CMOS,

  [D] consistency: 44 deg / 2592 px = 0.296 mrad/px vs quoted 0.28 -- agrees.

3d.  UNRESOLVED: INCIDENCE-ANGLE DROPOUT
  [X] No paper I opened states an explicit incidence-angle or slope cutoff for
      a landing altimeter.
  DERIVABLE SUBSTITUTE, which I would write in the thesis instead of inventing
  a threshold:
  [D] OLA Table 3 performance is specified against a 3 % Lambertian surface and
      Table 1 requires 3 dB of link margin at maximum range.  Lambertian return
      scales as cos(i).  3 dB = a factor of 2, so cos(i) = 0.5, i.e.
            i ~ 60 deg  exhausts the margin AT MAXIMUM RANGE,
      and the tolerable incidence angle relaxes as the range closes.
      So a 60 deg dropout at long range, loosening at short range, is traceable
      to a published link budget rather than invented.

4.  DIRECT VELOCITY MEASUREMENT -- NASA NAVIGATION DOPPLER LIDAR (NDL)
================================================================================
  This answers the open question "can we get velocity from lidar + camera?".
  The answer is yes, and the instrument has flown.

SOURCES
  (i)  Amzajerdian, Hines, Pierrottet, Barnes, Petway, Carson III,
       "Navigation Doppler Lidar for autonomous ground, aerial, and space
       vehicles", NASA NTRS 20200002534
       https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002534/downloads/20200002534.pdf
  (ii) Gragossian, Pierrottet, Estes, Barnes, Amzajerdian, Hines,
       "Navigation Doppler Lidar Performance at High Speed and Long Range",
       NASA NTRS 20200003107
       https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200003107/downloads/20200003107.pdf

  [Q] (i) "The NDL in its current configuration transmits three laser beams at
       different pointing angles toward the ground to measure range and
       velocity along each beam using a frequency modulated continuous wave
       (FMCW) technique.  The three line-of-sight measurements are then
       combined in order to determine the three components of the vehicle
       velocity vector and its altitude relative to the ground."
  [Q] (i) "Operating from over four kilometers altitude, the NDL obtains
       velocity and range precision measurements reaching 2 cm/sec and 2
       meters, respectively, dominated by the vehicle motion."
  [Q] (ii) "each measuring both range and velocity at 20 Hz"
  [Q] (ii) "The resolutions of LOS velocity and range measurements are
       0.2 cm/sec and 25 cm."
  [Q] (ii) "Each beam is expected to reach at least 4000 m in range, depending
       on atmospheric conditions and ground albedo, and up to 218 m/s in
       velocity."
  [Q] (ii) validated on a rocket sled at China Lake SNORT from <1 cm/s to
       218 m/s at distances greater than 4 km; compared against a video
       monitoring system and Doppler radar with "excellent agreement".
  [Q] (i) advantages over radar, verbatim: "the laser-based NDL sensor does not
       suffer from measurement perturbation from terrain features or signal
       ambiguity from transmitted side lobes, and is far less susceptible to
       signal clutter, such as returns from the lander structure or jettisoned
       vehicle components"
  [S] flight heritage: flown as a payload on Intuitive Machines IM-1 (landing
      22 Feb 2024) and "provided valid measurements at ranges that exceeded
      preflight expectations" (NTRS 20250008015, abstract level only)

  WHY THIS MATTERS TO US
  Three LOS beams give a DIRECT 3-axis velocity measurement at 20 Hz.  That
  removes the bias-velocity degeneracy outright instead of hoping a single
  nadir range channel plus bearings makes the accelerometer bias observable.
  Note 0.2 cm/s resolution vs the 6.04 m/s of velocity error an unestimated
  300 ug bias would build up -- three orders of magnitude of authority.

8.  DECISIONS REQUIRED BEFORE ANY CODE IS WRITTEN
================================================================================
  D1  ALTIMETER ARCHITECTURE
      (a) Hayabusa2 LIDAR alone: covers 30 m -- 25 km, so one instrument spans
          the whole descent, but 1 Hz and +/-1 to 5.5 m.
      (b) LIDAR above 4 km + NDL below 4 km: 20 Hz, direct 3-axis velocity,
          2 cm/s.  Matches the flown pattern of handing over to a short-range
          sensor (Hayabusa2 does exactly this at ~25 m with the LRF).
      My recommendation: (b).  It is the difference between arguing that the
      accelerometer bias is observable and simply measuring the velocity.

  D2  NDL YES / NO.  See D1(b).  If no, the bias observability argument has to
      be carried entirely by the altimeter's single nadir channel plus camera
      bearings, and the 490 s blind gap between camera activations stays a
      real weakness.

  D3  INCIDENCE-ANGLE DROPOUT at 60 deg, derived from the OLA 3 dB link margin
      as in section 3d -- accept, or model it differently?

  D4  IMU BIAS MODEL: random constant (tau -> infinity, the literal reading of
      "bias repeatability") or finite tau declared as an assumption?

  D5  FILTER FRAME.  Separate from the literature question but still open: the
      EKF in ekf.py currently propagates in the ROTATING frame while the IMU
      and the established frame convention are INERTIAL.  This must be fixed
      regardless of which sensor set is chosen.

  D6  CAMERA MODEL.  Replace the current "camera returns a position fix" with
