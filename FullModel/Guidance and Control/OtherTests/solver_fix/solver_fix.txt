SCvx SOLVER DIAGNOSIS AND FIX
=============================
2026-09-21.  All runs in FullModel/Guidance and Control/OtherTests/solver_fix/.
Test problem: the nominal descent, descent_nominal.npz, x_init 11 771 m from the landing site,
Mwet 599.5 kg, Mdry 490 kg, Tmax 20 N, Tmin 5 N, polyhedral gravity (ver64q, 49 152 facets).


1. WHAT WAS WRONG
-----------------
Three separate defects, all in the trust-region machinery. Together they meant the solver did not
return the solution to the problem it was given -- it returned a point that depended on the guess
it was started from, and then certified that point as converged.

The test that exposes it is invariance. Fix x_init, change nothing about the problem, and vary only
the flight time the warm start is seeded with. An optimiser must return one answer:

    seed t_go     returned t_go    fuel      label          (BEFORE)
      1440.5 s        1521.5 s     9.2119    stalled
      1620.6 s        1656.2 s     7.6263    stalled
      1710.6 s        1728.0 s     7.0483    stalled
      1800.7 s        1801.3 s     5.7853    converged
      1890.7 s        1864.2 s     5.8079    stalled
      1980.7 s        1917.6 s     5.7610    stalled
      2160.8 s        2092.2 s     6.1162    stalled
                      ^^^^^^^^ spread 266.6 s

The returned answer is the seed plus a bounded drift (+81, +36, +17, +1, -27, -63, -69 s). It always
moves toward the true optimum and always runs out of road before reaching it.


DEFECT 1 -- the trust-region upper caps sat BELOW the radii the run starts from.

  driver.py line 50:  ETA_MIN, ETA_MAX, ES_MIN, ES_MAX, EU_MIN, EU_MAX
                          = 1e-3, 1e6, 1.0, 200.0, 1e-5, 0.05
  shipped tune.py:    ETA_S0 = 500      against ES_MAX = 200
                      ETA_U0 = 0.1      against EU_MAX = 0.05

  The grow branch is  p["eta_s"] = min(p["eta_s"] * bex, ES_MAX).  With eta_s = 500 and a cap of
  200 that expression returns 200. A SUCCESSFUL step therefore SHRANK the region by 2.5x. The time
  and control regions could only ever decay, never recover, so rho >= r2t was silently a no-op.

  Measured, warm-started from the exact solution: eta_s grew 0 times in 14 iterations.

  Figures&data/solver_fix.png, right panel, is defect 2 rather than this one, but the two compound:
  defect 1 removed the region's ability to recover, defect 2 removed its reason to.


DEFECT 2 -- rho is a defect-contraction ratio, but its thresholds are calibrated as though it were
            a classic model-quality ratio.

  dL = Jsocp - J_ref is dominated by the exact-penalty term Wvc * ||d_ref||_1. Verified directly:
  dL = 32.3123 against Wvc * sum|d_ref| = 32.3123 (ratio 1.0001).

  The reason it matters is that this quantity does NOT shrink with the trust region. The subproblem
  can always drive its virtual control to zero, because the trajectory correction needed to do so is
  orders of magnitude smaller than eta. So dL is flat in eta over the entire useful range. Measured
  on a cold solve, iterations 4 to 9, all rejected so the reference is frozen:

      it    eta_s      eta       dL            rho
       4   500.00  1000.00   27525.058     -37.852
       5   250.00  1000.00   27525.058      -5.286
       6   125.00   500.00   27525.058      -2.144
       7    62.50   250.00   27525.058      -2.873
       8    31.25   125.00   27525.058      -2.418
       9    15.62    62.50   27525.058      -0.276

  dL is identical to three decimal places while eta_s falls 32x and eta falls 16x. A trust-region
  method requires the predicted decrease to vanish as the radius does; here it does not. What rho
  actually measures is

      rho  ~  1 - ||d_new||_1 / ||d_ref||_1

  the fraction of the reference defect the nonlinear propagation removed. Against that meaning the
  shipped thresholds read: grow only if the step cut the defect TENFOLD (r2t = 0.90), shrink unless
  it cut it by a third (r1t = 0.36). The first essentially never fires, the second fires on roughly
  every other accepted step. The region ratchets down.

  And it ratchets down while the step is still binding. Every iteration rides the trust-region
  boundary (tr_sigma_frac ~ 1.00), so t_go advances 100 + 100 + 50 + 50 + 25 + 25 + ... -- a
  geometric series whose SUM IS FINITE and, from a seed a few hundred seconds out, shorter than the
  distance left to travel. The run halts where the trust region ran out, not where the optimum is.


DEFECT 3 -- that halt was then certified as convergence.

  Once the region reaches its floors (ES_MIN = 1.0 s, EU_MIN = 1e-5) the six step-size convergence
  criteria are satisfied trivially: the steps are small because the solver is not allowed to take
  large ones, not because the iterate has stopped moving. Every row of the table above labelled
  "converged" before the fix is this.


  A WORD ON SCATTER. Repeated runs of the same configuration in different processes move by a few
  tens of seconds in t_go and a few per mil in fuel -- a known property of this solver, recorded
  separately and not resolved here. Every individual cell in the tables below therefore wobbles at
  that level. The spreads being compared (363 s against 97 s, 3.36% against 0.13%) are one to two
  orders of magnitude larger than the scatter, so the conclusions hold, but no single cell should be
  read as exact.


2. THE FIX
----------
driver.py (backup: driver.py.bak_presolverfix)

  a) eta_max / es_max / eu_max are now read from cfg and clamped to be at least the radius the run
     starts from, so a cap can never sit under its own initial value.

  b) the grow branch uses those locals instead of the module constants.

  c) a cfg flag `cap_clamp` (default True) selects the clamped caps; setting it False restores the
     original behaviour exactly, so the defect stays reproducible instead of having to be taken on
     trust. Diagnostic only -- never set it in flight.

  d) a cfg flag `shrink_on_accept` was added to allow sizing the region by step quality alone
     (shrink only on rejection). It was tested and left at the default True -- it made matters
     worse on its own (sigma spread 170.9 s against 104.7 s), because without the threshold
     recalibration below it simply buys more rejected steps.

Configuration -- the substantive change. Against rho's real meaning:

     r1t = 0.0     shrink only if the step made the defect WORSE
     r2t = 0.5     grow if the step at least HALVED it
     eta0   = 300 m       state trust region
     eta_s0 = 400 s       time-dilation trust region
     eta_u0 = 1e-3        control trust region

  eta_s0 is the single most important number. At the shipped 0.5 s the sigma spread over the seed
  ladder is 266.6 s; at 50 s it is 15-33 s regardless of the thresholds; at 400 s it is 4.3 s.
  The shipped value of 500 s was never in effect -- defect 1 capped it at 200 on the first grow.

  eta_u0 is the second. It has to be small enough that rho stays informative and large enough that
  the control can still reach the bang-bang structure. 1e-5 scores well on a test that hands the
  solver the optimal control already (it "wins" by freezing U) and collapses on any test that does
  not; 1e-2 and above lose 3-4% of the fuel. 1e-3 is the interior optimum.


3. WHAT IT BOUGHT
-----------------
Same seed ladder (Figures&data/solver_fix.png, left panel). `cap_clamp=False` restores the original
caps exactly, so "before" here is the original solver and not the patched one wearing old constants:

    seed t_go        BEFORE (original)           AFTER (patched + chosen)
                  t_go      fuel             t_go      fuel
      1440.5 s   2035.5 s   5.8515          1897.4 s   5.6614
      1620.6 s   2047.6 s   5.8002          1881.2 s   5.6740
      1710.6 s   2087.0 s   5.8674          1890.7 s   5.6681
      1800.7 s   1801.2 s   5.7863          1800.3 s   5.7817
      1890.7 s   2164.1 s   6.0881          1884.5 s   5.6970
      1980.7 s   2147.7 s   5.9822          1888.0 s   5.6774
      2160.8 s   2153.4 s   5.9401          1881.7 s   5.6759
                 spread 362.9 s             six of seven within 16.2 s (1881.2-1897.4)

Before, the answer is the seed: every run halts near where its trust region gave out, spanning
362.9 s and 0.30 kg of fuel. After, six of the seven seeds agree to 16.2 s and 0.036 kg, and they
agree on a BETTER trajectory than the one the nominal represents -- about 1887 s at 5.67 kg against
1801 s at 5.79 kg, 2.2% less fuel.

Separating the two fixes, on the same ladder (step24_truebefore.py):

    driver      constants   t_go spread   fuel range       eta_s grew   converged
    original    shipped        507.1 s    5.7847-6.5602        1          6/7
    patched     shipped        362.0 s    5.7847-6.1055        3          7/7
    patched     chosen          93.5 s    5.6592-5.7817       14          7/7

The cap fix on its own is worth little: it restores the region's ABILITY to grow (1 growth event
across seven runs becomes 3) but not its reason to, because r2t = 0.90 still demands a tenfold
defect cut. Recalibrating the thresholds to what rho actually measures is what turns 3 growth
events into 14 and collapses the spread.

The seventh is worth stating plainly rather than averaging away: the seed placed exactly on the
shipped nominal converges back to the shipped nominal, in 7 iterations. That point is a genuine
local optimum and the solver is entitled to stay there; it is reached only from a seed that starts
on it, and any other seed leaves it for the better one. It is also why section 5 warns against
reading the nominal as optimal.

On the earlier ladder run with eta_u0 = 1e-4 the same test gave a 4.3 s spread over five seeds at a
mean of 8.2 iterations; 1e-3 was preferred on the truth-scored battery below, which the seed ladder
alone cannot judge because it hands every run the optimal control already.

Optimality, scored on TRUTH rather than on what the solver reports. The control each run returns is
flown through DOP853 at rtol 1e-12, restarted at every ZOH node, and the fuel and terminal miss are
read off that propagation -- a number the solver cannot game by leaving a defect behind. Nine
problems: the nominal IC from four initialisations, two position dispersions, three mid-flight
restarts at 25 / 50 / 75 % along.

    config                                          worst gap   mean gap   worst flown miss
    baseline (eta_s0 500, eta_u0 0.1, r 0.36/0.90)      3.36%      1.85%        9.6 mm
    eta_s0 400, eta_u0 1e-3, r 0.0/0.30                 0.60%      0.21%        9.9 mm
    eta_s0 400, eta_u0 1e-3, r 0.0/0.50   <-- chosen    0.13%      0.03%        3.2 mm
    eta_s0 400, eta_u0 3e-3, r 0.0/0.30                 1.69%      0.39%       42.2 mm

Reported fuel equals flown fuel to within 1e-4 kg on every run, so nothing in this table is bought
with dynamic infeasibility.

Warm-started from the exact solution: 14 iterations before, 3 after.

Self-consistency. Re-solving warm-started from the solver's own previous answer, repeatedly -- a
converged solver is a fixed point of that map. After the fix it is one: fuel moves less than
0.001 kg and t_go less than 0.1 s between rounds, from the nominal and from a cold solve alike.
Before the fix this test could not be passed, because the answer depended on the guess.


4. HOW TO USE IT
----------------
Warm start everything from the nominal, t_go included. run_one takes the flight time from the guess
when tf is free (driver.py, `sigma = float(sw)`), and the ballistic estimate it falls back on
otherwise reads 1502.8 s against a true t_go of 1800.7 s -- 300 s short on the very problem the
constants were tuned for. guidance.py now seeds the first cycle from descent_nominal.npz and scales
its time-dilation region and tf tolerance against the warm t_go rather than the ballistic estimate.

The dimensionless constants in guidance.py were recalibrated to match:

    ETA0_ND    = 0.025487     300 m   / 11 771 m
    ETA_S0_ND  = 0.222136     400 s   / 1800.7 s     (scaled by the warm t_go, not sigma0)
    ETA_U0_ND  = 0.029975     1e-3    / 0.033361
    RHO_SHRINK = 0.0
    RHO_GROW   = 0.5

persist_tr, which carried the trust region across guidance cycles, is now off by default. It was a
workaround for the ratchet -- the region could only decay, so re-opening it each cycle cost
iterations shrinking it back down. With the ratchet fixed at its cause, carrying a radius that one
cycle collapsed into the next is actively harmful.

Both defaults measured over six receding-horizon cycles, flying one 30 s control interval along the
plan between them (step22_persist.py):

    persist_tr   nominal seed   iterations per cycle      converged   t + t_go drift
      False         yes         11, 26, 28, 19, 28, 18       6/6          65.1 s
      False         no          60, 46, 43, 41, 42, 23       1/6         377.9 s
      True          yes         11, 21, 49, 33,  2,  1       1/6          42.7 s
      True          no          60, 23, 35, 32, 31, 26       0/6          25.7 s

The persist_tr = True row shows the pathology directly: by cycle 5 the carried region has collapsed,
the solve "finishes" in 2 and then 1 iteration, and those are false convergences of exactly the kind
section 1 describes. Without the nominal seed the first cycle exhausts its 60-iteration budget and
the planned mission duration wanders by 378 s over six cycles.


5. WHAT IS STILL NOT FIXED
--------------------------
The cold start. No configuration tested reaches the warm basin from the straight-line guess:

    cold, wide region      t_go 2060 s   flown fuel 6.006 kg
    cold, tuned region     stalls        flown fuel 8.304 kg
    two-stage, wide then tuned, stage 1 = 5/8/10/12/15/20 iterations
                           t_go 1978-2006 s, 5.816-5.946 kg, i.e. +2.6% to +4.8%
    warm from the nominal  t_go 1884 s   flown fuel 5.658 kg

This is basin selection, not trust-region sizing: stage 1 commits to a t_go near 2000 s within its
first four iterations and the refinement is local, so no amount of stage-2 effort recovers. The
practical consequence is small -- a cold start is needed once, to produce a nominal -- but it means
the nominal should not be taken as optimal merely because the solver converged to it. The shipped
nominal (1800.7 s, 5.785 kg) is itself about 2.2% off the best trajectory found here (1884 s,
5.658 kg), which the fixed solver reaches when warm-started from it.

Solve time is 20 s per guidance cycle at K = 80 nodes. That is a separate question from correctness
and has not been addressed here.


6. THE CLOSED LOOP, AND TWO FAULTS THAT WERE NOT THE SOLVER
-----------------------------------------------------------
Re-running closed_loop.py --start-frac 0.75 with the fixed solver exposed cycles that ran for tens
of minutes while their neighbours took seconds. Two of the three causes turned out to be outside the
solver entirely, which is worth recording because the symptom pointed straight at it.

  (a) INFEASIBLE SUBPROBLEM, TREATED AS A BAD STEP.  The driver responded to a subproblem it could
      not solve by shrinking the trust region and trying again -- 120 times. Shrinking makes the
      feasible set SMALLER; it cannot repair infeasibility. Fixed: if every solver reports
      "infeasible", stop immediately with reason "infeasible".

  (b) THE WARM t_go WAS OUTSIDE THE FLIGHT-TIME BOUNDS.  sigma is a hard bound in the subproblem and
      driver.py took it from the warm start raw. At a real MPC state 1005 m from the site the bounds
      were [313.8, 691.8] s, and guidance.py's fallback handed over the WHOLE nominal -- 1800.7 s --
      whenever the previous cycle had failed. Iteration 0 was then infeasible, both solvers agreed,
      and because the fallback fires exactly when the previous cycle failed, one bad cycle became a
      death spiral. Measured at that state:

          warm t_go 1800.7 s  (full nominal)   infeasible, 0 iterations
          warm t_go  310.5 s  (nominal tail)   solves, 24 iterations
          warm t_go  186.3 s  (0.6x)           infeasible  -- below tf_min = 313.8 s
          warm t_go  565.7 s  (1.8x)           converged, 8 iterations

      Two fixes: driver.py widens tf_min/tf_max to admit a warm t_go rather than rejecting it (the
      ballistic bounds assume the vehicle starts at rest, so on a mid-flight restart they can exclude
      the trajectory it is actually flying -- tf_min was 313.8 s against a nominal t_go of 310.5 s at
      that range), and guidance.py seeds from the nominal TAIL, matched on remaining range.

  (c) A CHATTERING RIGHT-HAND SIDE IN THE TEST ITSELF -- not the solver at all.  closed_loop.py
      carried the actuator as an ODE state, which means writing the slew rate limiter as a
      differential equation. A rate limiter is not a differential equation, it is a switch: turning
      at the full slew rate until the error reaches zero and then stopping means the direction
      overshoots and reverses, i.e. a sliding mode. DOP853 at rtol 1e-12 cannot resolve an infinitely
      fast oscillation, so it drove the step toward machine precision. Measured, one 30 s arc:

          cycle 0   0.3 s      824 gravity calls
          cycle 1 225.3 s  518 639 gravity calls      <- same arc length, 630x the work

      Softening the switch into a boundary layer helped but did not remove it, because
      ang = arccos(d . d_cmd) is not Lipschitz where it matters. The real fix is structural: the
      actuator is a zero-order-hold device and Actuator.step already advances it EXACTLY in closed
      form, so hold the thrust over a 0.5 s sub-step, advance the actuator analytically at the
      boundaries, and let the integrator see only the smooth translational dynamics.

      This was found with faulthandler.dump_traceback_later, not by reasoning -- the traceback was
      inside solve_ivp, never inside the solver. py-spy needs root on macOS; faulthandler does not.

  Also added: a wall-clock budget (cfg "max_seconds", 20 s in guidance.py). An iteration count does
  not bound time, because the cost of an iteration is not bounded. And the SCS fallback, which is
  only reached when CLARABEL has already failed, now carries max_iters and time_limit_secs.


7. INTEGRATOR FOR THE CLOSED LOOP: FIXED-STEP RK8
-------------------------------------------------
With the right-hand side smooth, the choice is about cost, not stability. On a single smooth 30 s arc
with an identical control, referenced to DOP853 at rtol 1e-13 (step29_rk8.py):

    method                calls    wall      pos err     vel err
    DOP853 rtol 1e-12        62    0.02     4.55e-13    4.37e-14
    DOP853 rtol 1e-10        50    0.02     9.17e-13    2.67e-14
    RK8 dt 1.000 s          360    0.12     9.37e-13    5.89e-14
    RK8 dt 0.500 s          720    0.25     4.90e-12    6.34e-14
    RK8 dt 0.250 s         1440    0.50     2.91e-12    5.57e-14

Every one of them is at round-off, so accuracy does not choose. On an isolated arc DOP853 is 6-30x
cheaper. Inside the loop the ranking REVERSES, and the reason is the sub-stepping: the actuator is
advanced every 0.5 s, so an adaptive integrator is restarted 60 times per guidance cycle and pays its
startup and error control on every restart -- 2820 calls per cycle, about 47 per sub-arc. A
fixed-step method has no restart cost, so one RK8 step per actuator sub-step costs 12.

    DOP853 rtol 1e-12, restarted every 0.5 s     2820 calls/cycle
    RK8 dt 0.25 s                                1440 calls/cycle
    RK8 dt 0.50 s  (one step per sub-step)        720 calls/cycle

RK8 at dt = dt_act = 0.5 s is the choice: 4x cheaper than the adaptive method, accurate to 5e-12 m,
and its cost is fixed in advance rather than data-dependent, which is what a Monte Carlo needs. A
fixed-step method has no event root-finder, so the touchdown crossing is bisected explicitly --
without that it reports touchdown wherever the grid falls, measured at 0.43 m below the surface.

A fourth fault, in the test rather than in any of the above: the actuator was initialised COLD --
0 N pointing at the local vertical -- even though the run picks up an existing descent three quarters
of the way down, where the engine is lit and already pointed. That injected a transient belonging to
no trajectory: a 15 N thrust step and a 139 deg pointing error that the 2 deg/s rate limit needs
~70 s to slew out, on an arc only ~430 s long, so the vehicle spent its first two guidance cycles
recovering from an initial condition that was never real. Initialising from the nominal's own control
at the handover node (5.00 N at 23.3 deg from local vertical, U[0:3] being an acceleration so scaled
by the node mass) removes it:

    actuator at t = 0        peak pointing error    propellant     miss
    cold, 0 N at vertical         139.13 deg          1.818 kg    1.93 m
    from the nominal node          16.57 deg          1.584 kg    2.79 m

The remaining 16.57 deg is real: at t = 360 s the command steps 11 deg in one cycle and the slew
limit genuinely binds for ~5.5 s. The 1 deg spikes at every other cycle boundary are the same effect
in miniature. Propellant falls 13% once the spurious transient is gone, which is the measure of how
much that initial condition was costing.

Three landings, same initial state, 75% along the nominal:

    integrator              cycles   touchdown    miss     speed    propellant
    DOP853 rtol 1e-12         14      418.68 s   1.390 m  0.943 m/s   1.955 kg
    RK8 dt 0.25 s             14      398.24 s   3.018 m  2.105 m/s   1.609 kg
    RK8 dt 0.50 s             15      424.11 s   3.881 m  1.206 m/s   1.908 kg

All three land. They differ by more than the integrators do -- the integrators agree to 1e-12 m on an
identical control, so the spread is the solver's run-to-run scatter amplified through feedback: at
cycle 10 one run commanded 18.54 N and another 5.00 N from a similar state. The closed loop is
therefore sensitive to that scatter, and a single run is not a measurement. This is the same scatter
recorded before this work and is NOT resolved by it.

The terminal phase is the remaining weak spot: the last two or three cycles hit the 20 s budget or
the iteration cap ("timeout/maxiter", "stalled/maxiter"), and touchdown speed lands between 0.9 and
2.1 m/s depending on the run. A dedicated terminal-phase treatment is the obvious next step and has
not been attempted here.


8. WHY IT WAS LANDING AT 1.47 m/s
----------------------------------
The terminal speed constraint is VTOUCH = 0.30 m/s and the nominal meets it exactly. The closed loop
was arriving at 1.47 m/s. The cause is structural, not a tuning error.

MINIMUM FUEL MEANS NO MARGIN. With Tmax = 20 N on ~594 kg the net deceleration is 0.028 m/s^2, so
stopping from 4.3 m/s needs ~330 m. The fuel-optimal solution brakes as late as physics allows, and
the nominal's own margin is correspondingly thin:

    nominal node 27   altitude 244.18 m   stopping distance 236.80 m   margin  7.4 m
    nominal node 28   altitude  70.19 m   stopping distance  68.73 m   margin  1.5 m

THE SWITCH FALLS INSIDE A GUIDANCE CYCLE. Minimum-fuel control is bang-bang. MPC conventionally
applies u(tau = 0) and holds it for the whole cycle, so a switch in the interior of the cycle is
deferred by up to one full cycle. At 4.4 m/s a 10 s cycle covers 44 m of altitude while the stopping
distance falls only ~6 m, so the margin burns at ~3.5 m per second: the 11.5 m available at cycle 27
survives about three seconds. Measured, with a 10 s cycle:

    cycle 27   alt 356.02 m   |v| 4.396   T  5.00 N   needs 344.52 m   margin  +11.50 m
    cycle 28   alt 314.81 m   |v| 4.357   T  5.00 N   needs 338.41 m   margin  -23.60 m
    cycle 29   alt 274.65 m   |v| 4.163   T 15.02 N   needs 308.94 m   margin  -34.29 m

The vehicle was still coasting at Tmin one full cycle AFTER the landing had become unrecoverable,
and every cycle after that commanded full thrust and could not recover. The plans reporting t_go of
25-93 s from those states were claiming landings Tmax cannot deliver -- path and terminal constraints
are penalised rather than hard, so the subproblem buys the shortfall with slack instead of reporting
infeasibility. That is worth remembering when reading any "converged" label near the surface.

Touchdown speed scaled straight with the cycle length, which is the signature of a one-cycle delay:

    guidance cycle    touchdown speed    miss      solves   wall
       30 s               1.47 m/s      2.79 m       14     1.5 min
       10 s               0.84 m/s      0.62 m       43     3.1 min
        5 s               0.52 m/s      0.12 m       86     5.8 min

THE FIX IS TO STOP DISCARDING THE PLAN. The plan already carries the switch at its own resolution --
K = 80 nodes over a ~60 s terminal horizon is about 1 s -- and U is a zero-order hold on THAT grid,
not on the guidance grid. Following it across the cycle instead of holding node 0 (plan_thrust in
closed_loop.py; --hold-first restores the conventional behaviour) removes the cycle-length
sensitivity entirely:

    mode                cycle   touchdown    miss     solves   wall
    hold u(tau=0)        30 s    1.47 m/s   2.79 m      14    1.5 min
    hold u(tau=0)         5 s    0.52 m/s   0.12 m      86    5.8 min
    follow the plan      30 s    0.53 m/s   0.54 m      15    1.4 min
    follow the plan      10 s    0.51 m/s   0.31 m      43    3.1 min

A 30 s cycle now performs like a 5 s one at a sixth of the solves.

WHAT IS LEFT. Touchdown sits at ~0.51-0.54 m/s against VTOUCH = 0.30 and no longer improves with a
shorter cycle, so the remaining gap is a floor from something else -- the candidates are the actuator
lag and slew limit, which the optimiser does not model at all, and the terminal-phase solves still
hitting the iteration cap or the wall-clock budget. Not chased here.
